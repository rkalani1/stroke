Key Principles
Presenting SBP 150-219: target 140 and maintain 130-150. Presenting SBP ≥220: reduce about 20%, never more than 25%, in the first hour, then gradually reduce to 140-160. Presenting SBP <150: do not actively lower to 140.
Start BP treatment as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours of onset, and reach the selected target about 1 hour after treatment begins.
Obtain rapid agent-specific assessment using INR, thrombin time, direct-Xa or anti-Xa testing, and last-dose timing; follow the applicable reversal gate below.
Use the source-defined Neurosurgery, hydrocephalus, mass-effect, MIE, and research screens below; do not infer an operative pathway from location, volume, or GCS alone.

•

•
Notify the receiving ED, stroke service, and ICU admitting service for ICU-level admissions.
ED diagnosis or arrival
•
Measure hematoma volume using ABC/2 and treat >=15 mL as the early Neurosurgery + stroke-service evaluation threshold.
•
ED clinicians or the stroke service may consult Neurosurgery directly; prior approval is not required.
•
Whichever service calls Neurosurgery closes the loop with the designated on-call stroke attending and other involved service so the plan is shared and documented.
•
Consult earlier at any size for IVH, hydrocephalus, cerebellar hemorrhage, mass effect, neurologic decline, multicompartmental hemorrhage, vascular lesion concern, ED attending discretion, or clinician concern.
CTA/MRA branch
•
If CTA/MRA shows an underlying vascular lesion or anomaly, the Neurosurgery/neurointerventional pathway leads admission or operative/interventional planning with neurocritical care or ICU support.
•
If there is no vascular lesion and no surgical pathway, the stroke service leads admission with ICU support as needed.
Monitoring adjuncts
•
Use close serial neurologic exams and repeat imaging when the exam changes.

MINUTE screen
Age 18-80; Spontaneous non-traumatic supratentorial non-thalamic basal-ganglia IPH; Basal-ganglia IPH volume >=20 mL by ABC/2; NIHSS >=6; CTA/MRA without vascular lesion; Arrival <=15 hours since last known well; No clear standard-of-care surgical indication

MIRROR registry screen
Selected spontaneous supratentorial IPH being considered for minimally invasive evacuation; Volume, NIHSS, premorbid mRS, and GCS thresholds are version-sensitive and must be checked against the active registry protocol before use; The latest criteria would loosen the prior screen; do not apply a revised executable screen until protocol-owner sign-off; No underlying vascular lesion; Potential MIS timing within 24 hours of last known well or qualifying wake-up hemorrhage window
.
Do not let registry screening delay urgent surgical, trial, or stabilization decisions. Deep IPH considered for minimally invasive surgery outside the trial and registry pathways, or beyond 24 hours from last known well, requires discussion with both the on-call stroke attending and the inpatient neurology attending before proceeding.

Use the presenting-SBP branches: 150-219 → target 140 and maintain 130-150; ≥220 → reduce about 20% but never more than 25% in the first hour, then gradually reduce to 140-160; <150 → do not actively lower to 140.

ICH Volume Calculation:
ABC/2 Method:
A = largest ICH diameter (cm)
B = largest diameter 90 degrees to A on same slice (cm)
C = (# CT slices with ICH) x (slice thickness)
Volume = A x B x C / 2
Anticoagulation Reversal
Warfarin
Immediate:
Vitamin K 10 mg IV for all warfarin patients.
INR ≥2.0 (COR/LOE 1/B):
4F-PCC (Kcentra) 2000 units IV immediately. Vitamin K 10 mg IV is also given immediately.
INR 1.6-1.9 (COR 2b, LOE C-LD):
4F-PCC 2000 units IV may be reasonable.
INR 1.3-1.5 (COR 2b, LOE C-LD):
4F-PCC 2000 units IV may be reasonable; decide case by case.
Fixed-dose caveat:
the institutional 2000-unit dose may underdose heavier or high-INR patients. Kcentra label dosing: INR 2-<4, 25 units/kg (max 2500); INR 4-6, 35 units/kg (max 3500); INR >6, 50 units/kg (max 5000).
Check INR:
at 30 min, then every 6h for 24h after PCC.
INR >1.5 after PCC:
page hematology and consider an additional 500 units PCC or 2-4 units plasma (FFP).
INR >1.5 at 24h:
repeat vitamin K 10 mg IV over 30 min.
FFP pathway (if PCC unavailable or contraindicated):
4 units emergency-release plasma immediately and request an additional 4 units (ideal plasma dose 15 mL/kg; each unit is 250-300 mL, so some patients need additional units) → recheck INR → if >1.5 administer 4 more units → if still >1.5 consult hematology. If INR is >1.5 at 24 hours, repeat vitamin K 10 mg IV over 30 min. Consider concurrent furosemide if history of CHF.

Heparins
Treatment-dose UFH (COR 2a, LOE C-LD):
protamine 1 mg per 100 units of UFH given in the prior 2-3 hours (max 50 mg) by slow IV injection; if the aPTT remains elevated, consider 0.5 mg per 100 units (NCS/SCCM 2016).
UFH — HIT screen:
if platelets <100 K/µL, send heparin-induced platelet antibodies; if positive, consult hematology.
Treatment-dose LMWH — enoxaparin, dalteparin, or tinzaparin (COR 2b, LOE C-LD):
enoxaparin within 8 h → protamine 1 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin (max 50 mg); 8-12 h → 0.5 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin; dalteparin/tinzaparin → 1 mg per 100 anti-Xa units given within 3-5 half-lives (max 50 mg). Protamine only partially reverses LMWH (NCS/SCCM 2016).
Fondaparinux and Parenteral Direct Thrombin Inhibitors
Stop fondaparinux, bivalirudin, or argatroban and obtain STAT hematology-attending consultation.
No specific antidote is available for these agents.
For bivalirudin or argatroban, repeat the DTI level at 60 minutes after stopping; if it remains prolonged, consult the hematology attending.
Common Reversal Monitoring
Laboratory testing: baseline emergency stroke panel; repeat the STAT panel 15-30 minutes after reversal including the agent-specific assay (PT/INR for warfarin, thrombin time for dabigatran, anti-Xa for factor-Xa inhibitors); PT/INR and CBC at 6 hours; PT/INR, CBC and fibrinogen at 24 hours.
CT head: baseline, 6 hours or sooner for clinical deterioration, and 24 hours.
Neurologic checks every hour for 24 hours; blood pressure per the ICH protocol; monitor for thrombotic complications.
Blood-Product Replacement Thresholds
Request these products, if not already ordered under a pathway above, based on the STAT results and assuming an average-sized adult.
Fibrinogen <125 mg/dL:
prepare and transfuse 2 pre-pooled units of cryoprecipitate (each pool ≈5 single-donor units; total ≈10 units).
Platelets <50 K/µL:
prepare and transfuse 2 units of platelets.
Platelets 50-100 K/µL:
prepare and transfuse 1 unit of platelets.
INR >1.5:
use the 4F-PCC or plasma pathway above.
These thrombocytopenia thresholds are distinct from antiplatelet-associated hemorrhage, where routine platelet transfusion is not recommended.
Absolute contraindications to PCC:
• Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
• Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)
Relative contraindications to PCC/FFP:
• Thrombotic event <6 weeks
• Major surgery ≤6 weeks
• Known prothrombotic disorder
• Mechanical circulatory support (involve cardiology)
Discuss relative contraindications with the stroke service before proceeding.
Antiplatelet-Associated ICH
Discontinue antiplatelet agent(s). Platelet transfusion is
potentially harmful
for aspirin-associated ICH when emergency surgery is not planned (ICH 2022, COR 3: Harm, LOE B-R); NCS/SCCM 2026 also suggests against platelet transfusion for antiplatelet-associated spontaneous IPH not requiring neurosurgery (conditional, low certainty).
Aspirin + emergency neurosurgery: platelet transfusion may be considered (ICH 2022, COR 2b, LOE C-LD; NCS/SCCM 2026 conditional recommendation for transfusion in spontaneous IPH on aspirin undergoing neurosurgery).
Desmopressin efficacy is uncertain for antiplatelet-associated hemorrhage (COR/LOE 2b/C-LD); the institutional source does not provide a dose.
Hematoma Expansion Prevention
Blood Pressure Target
Spontaneous ICH only. Not for hemorrhage from a vascular malformation or for post-operative neurosurgical cases.
Presenting SBP 150-219:
target 140 and maintain 130-150.
Presenting SBP ≥220:
reduce by about 20%, never more than 25%, in the first hour; then gradually reduce to 140-160, and after at least 6 hours with a repeat head CT transition to the 130-150 target.
Presenting SBP <150:
do not actively lower to 140.
Timing:
start as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours of onset, and reach the selected target about 1 hour after treatment begins.
Process:
use smooth, sustained control and avoid peaks or variability.
Lower boundary:
avoid iatrogenic SBP <130 during the first 24 hours. Spontaneous autoregulation below 130 is acceptable without symptomatic hypotension; vasopressors require agreement from the stroke and neurocritical-care attendings.
Large/severe ICH or decompression:
individualize and consider an initial SBP 160-180, reassessing after about 6 hours or repeat CT.
Renal/ICP considerations:
use caution with aggressive lowering in moderate-severe renal dysfunction and maintain CPP >60 mmHg, especially with elevated ICP.
Agents named by the institutional source:
IV nicardipine or IV labetalol; no doses are supplied.
